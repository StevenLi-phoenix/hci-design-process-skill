# Interview guide — <topic>

Problem statement (1–2 lines): <…>
Format: semi-structured · <30> min · in person at <their setting> / live video · interviewers: <lead>, <note-taker>

## 1. Introduction (≈2 min)
"Hi <first name>, thanks for meeting. I'm <name> and this is <name>, who'll take notes. We're researching <topic> to understand people's experiences — there are no right or wrong answers. It takes about <30> minutes. May we record audio? It's only used by our team for this research, and we won't use your full name. You can skip any question or stop at any time. Any questions before we start?"

## 2. Warm-up (≈3 min)
- What does a typical day look like for you?
- <easy, relevant context question>

## 3. Main body — themes derived from the problem statement
### Theme A: <name>
- Tell me about the last time you <did the task>. Walk me through it from the start.
- <follow-up probes: What happened next? Why? Can you show me? What did you use?>
- <alternate phrasing of the key question>
### Theme B: <name>
- …
### Theme C: <name>
- …
### (Theme D: <name>)
- …
(Order: specific past incidents first, opinions/comparisons later, speculative last. ~25 questions total is fine.)

## 4. Wrap-up (≈3 min)
- Any suggestions or last thoughts on <topic>?
- **Is there anything we didn't talk about that you think is important?**
- Thank you — <contact details / next steps>.

## Pre-flight checklist
- [ ] Every question is open-ended (no yes/no), non-leading, not hypothetical, not "what features would you like"
- [ ] Lint pass: no question or probe opens with Do/Did/Is/Are/Was/Were/Have/Has/Would/Could/Don't/Wouldn't (except the closing "Is there anything we didn't talk about…?") — rewrite as What/How/Why/Tell me about/Walk me through
- [ ] No "how often/how long in general" estimates — ask about specific occasions instead
- [ ] Key questions phrased 2–3 ways
- [ ] Piloted on one person
- [ ] Recording permission + anonymization plan ready; note sheet for location, people present, artifacts
